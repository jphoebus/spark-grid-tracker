What stands out

Headquarters are not project sites

The 26 locations in DOE's list are where prime applicants are based, including Guam and California, where EPRI is headquartered even though its project runs on FirstEnergy and Salt River Project systems. Several projects cross state lines; Basin Electric's covers six states.

The data center story is narrower than the headlines

Seven of the 31 project descriptions cite data centers, large loads, or new load, and only two name data centers directly. Most describe congestion, reliability, and aging infrastructure. Large loads are driving the urgency, but much of the work serves the whole system.

States are applicants, not just regulators

Four state-led projects in Colorado, Oklahoma, Ohio, and Indiana request $810 million, 43 percent of all federal funds. One of them is led by Ohio's utility commission.

Utilities are bringing more than the minimum

Six smart grid projects request exactly $50 million in federal funds, and several put in far more of their own. Alabama Power's cost share is 73 percent; the New York Power Authority's is 81 percent.

What to watch

  • Selections become signed awards between October 2026 and January 2027. Final amounts may differ from these requests.
  • How each project's cost share is recovered: through federally regulated transmission rates, state-regulated retail rates, or cooperative and public power boards.
  • Pennsylvania's two projects: PPL's Montour corridor rebuild and Duquesne Light's GRID-SURGE interconnection upgrades.
  • How the four state-led projects coordinate work across multiple utilities and grid regions.

The question that will shape the next phase: who gets the capacity these upgrades unlock, the large loads driving demand or the customers paying for congestion today, and who decides.
